## 1. Chinese-language studies on the Black Death and religion and society

### Takeaway
Mainland Chinese work on the Black Death is real and published in top journals, including 《中国社会科学》, 《历史研究》 and 《世界历史》. It comes overwhelmingly from one scholar, 李化成 (Li Huacheng, Shaanxi Normal University), and his circle. It focuses on England and on medical and social history. Religious topics appear in three items: the English Church (2008), the flagellants (2013), and monastic change and collective flagellation (2024). In Taiwan's history journals I found no peer-reviewed article on the Black Death. Taiwanese engagement is popular or essayistic, for example 李尚仁's 2020 piece.

## 2. Chinese-language studies on African American slave religion, the Black church, spirituals and Black theology

### Takeaway
This literature is **very thin**. I found no Chinese-language historical monograph or peer-reviewed historical article on antebellum slave religion, nothing like a Sinophone Raboteau. What exists falls into four groups:
1. Translated theology, chiefly Pinn's *Why, Lord?* (Tao Fong Shan, 2006).
2. Survey chapters on "Black theology" in Chinese theology textbooks.
3. Musicological or literary articles on spirituals in mainland teaching-oriented journals.
4. One Taiwanese MA thesis comparing spirituals with Taiwanese hymns in civil-rights contexts.

I found no Chinese translation of James Cone's major works.

### Inferences

- Chinese engagement with Black religion runs mainly through theology textbooks and music pedagogy, not through history departments. A Taiwan-based historian of religion therefore has an open field.

### Gaps
- I found no Chinese translation of Raboteau's *Slave Religion*, Genovese's *Roll, Jordan, Roll*, Levine's *Black Culture and Black Consciousness*, or Du Bois's *The Souls of Black Folk* in this session. **[unverified, prior knowledge]** Du Bois's *Souls* does have mainland translations (e.g., 《黑人的灵魂》). - I could not search CNKI for 黑人神学 or 美国黑人宗教 dissertations, where mainland MA theses likely exist.

## 3. Chinese translations of key Western works

### Takeaway
Several key works have verified Chinese translations: McNeill, Moltmann, Pinn, and three Black Death histories (Byrne, Gasquet, Gottfried). Tuchman's *A Distant Mirror* exists in Chinese, though the translator was not confirmed. Weber, Berger, Taylor and Girard translations are well known but were **not re-verified** in this session; see Gaps. None of Ziegler, Aberth, Herlihy, Delumeau, Pargament or Norris & Inglehart was confirmed in translation.

## 6. Assessment: how rich is the Sinophone literature, and where are the gaps?

### Takeaway
The literature is **moderately rich but highly concentrated on the Black Death side**, essentially one productive mainland school. It is **very thin on the African American side**: theology translations and music essays, but no historical scholarship on slave religion. There appears to be **no Chinese-language comparative study of the two cases, and no Taiwanese peer-reviewed work on either as religious history.** That gap is a credible contribution claim for an NYCU-based author.

### Cited Findings
- **Black Death.** At least 13 journal items by Li Huacheng, published 2006–2024 in top PRC venues, three of them explicitly on Church and religious movements. — [SNNU page](https://his.snnu.edu.cn/info/1014/1591.htm)
- **Taiwan history journals.** A targeted search found no Black Death or flagellant article; the summary explicitly recommended checking tables of contents. — (search result summary, Section 1)
- **African American religion.** The only substantial item is a translated theodicy monograph (Pinn 2006). The only Taiwanese academic item is an MA thesis (Hsu 2022). — [校園書房](https://shop.campus.org.tw/ProductDetails.aspx?ProductID=000360319); [TGST IR](https://ir.taitheo.org.tw/bitstream/987654321/7411/2/M.A.CM+2022-3_%E5%BE%90%E6%AC%BD%E7%9B%9B.pdf)
- **Cone.** No Chinese translation of Cone's books was found; the Chinese titles on Wikipedia appear to be descriptive renderings. — [zh.wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E9%BB%91%E8%89%B2%E7%A5%9E%E5%AD%B8)

### Inferences
Possible contribution claims for the English article:
1. Sinophone Black Death historiography, led by Li Huacheng, independently documents institutional strain and anti-clerical lay religiosity in the flagellants, but it has not been read alongside African American religious history.
2. Chinese engagement with Black religion is theological (Pinn's humanist theodicy) rather than historical, so the "intensification" story of slave Christianity (Raboteau, Frey & Wood) has no Sinophone counterpart.
3. Taiwan offers its own suffering-and-faith cases (921, Morakot, the church under martial law) that would make a natural third comparison. The local literature on these is scattered across psychology and religious-studies venues, not history.

Suggested phrasing: "Sinophone scholarship has addressed each case separately and unevenly (e.g., Li 2008, 2013; Pinn 2006 [Chinese trans.]), but no Chinese-language study has compared them."
