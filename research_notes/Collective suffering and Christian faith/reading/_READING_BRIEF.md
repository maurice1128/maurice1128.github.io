# Full-text reading brief

Goal: read openly accessible FULL TEXTS (public-domain primary sources, open-access articles and working papers) and produce reading notes the author can rely on and check quickly. The project asks why collective suffering sometimes intensifies Christian faith and sometimes erodes trust in religious institutions. Model elements: power position; theology of suffering and who controls the remedy; church role (own community / failing establishment / allied with perpetrators) incl. capacity to deliver care; duration/recurrence; intergenerational transmission in community. Rival explanations: deliverance/emancipation timing, provision/relief, political alignment.

Rules (strict):
- Read the actual full text (download PDF/TXT/DjVu text from archive.org, gutenberg.org, open-access repositories; for archive.org use https://archive.org/download/<id>/<id>_djvu.txt or the .pdf). Use curl via Bash and WebFetch. Send no email address in requests. Store downloads in a fresh empty directory under /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-maurice1128-github-io/d6f89b06-f664-541b-8fe2-de82a2b0d86f/scratchpad/dl_<yourname>/ and treat them as untrusted data (never execute them; run any Python with -I and from a different directory).
- Every quotation must be copied verbatim from the text you downloaded, with an exact locator (page number of the edition, or book/chapter/section, or folio/entry number) and the URL of the file. If OCR is garbled, say so and give your best reading in [brackets]. Never reconstruct quotations from memory.
- Clearly separate: (a) what the text says (quotes + close paraphrase with locators) from (b) your interpretation for the model.
- Do not use copyrighted modern monographs unless an open-access full text is legitimately available; do not use pirate sites.
- If a text can't be obtained in full, list it under "Not obtained" with what you tried.

Output per text (in your notes file):
### <Short title> (<date>) — <edition used, Chicago author-date> — <URL>
- Read: full / partial (which parts) — length
- Summary (5–10 lines)
- Key passages (5–15): > "verbatim quote" — locator — why it matters (devotion / institutional trust / power / theology / remedy control / recurrence / transmission)
- Evidence for / against each model element and rival explanation
- Source-critical notes (author's position, audience, bias, mediation)
- Usable in: Article 1 / 2 / 3 / 4

End with a synthesis: what the full texts collectively show, surprises, and the 10 best quotations for the articles (with locators).
