How much joint-angle error can the identification of a weak muscle group tolerate? A predictive-simulation study

Motion capture is usually judged by its measurement error. This study judged it by a decision: in forward-dynamic gait simulation, where the weak muscle group is known, can that group still be identified once joint-angle error is added? Rank-1 identification fell from 0.845 at 0° to 0.549 at 12° and stayed above twice the majority-class baseline of 0.227, but mild (20 %) weakness was identified at only 0.610 even at 2°. Everything here is simulation; nothing was measured in patients.

Mu-Hua Wang · National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University · first author, with Hui-Ting Shih (advisor and corresponding author)

The 5° gain depends on how the feature sets are drawn

Six timing features in the camera set derive from vertical ground reaction force in this model, which a camera alone would not supply. The 27 purely kinematic features score 0.824, 0.875, 0.742, 0.623 and 0.536 at 0, 2, 5, 8 and 12°, and against the better of the two camera-only sets the 5° gain falls to +0.033, about twice the 0.015 that one condition changing its answer would move. 8° is the first level at which the gain holds under every way of drawing the sets; it is the level this design supports, not a purchasing threshold.

How it was tested

Simulation
SCONE 2.4.4 forward dynamics on the planar H0914 model (OpenSim 4 engine), Geyer–Herr reflex controller re-optimised by CMA-ES after each imposed weakness, so the walker adapts to it.
Design
5 muscle groups × 3 severities (20, 40, 60 % reduction in maximum isometric force, left limb) × 5 model variants = 75 cells; 71 passed quality control and 66 were analysed.
Variants
Whole-body rescalings of one model by a single strength factor (0.85, 0.95, 1.00, 1.05, 1.10). A variant is not a separate person.
Capture error
Zero-mean Gaussian error added to each of the 200 samples of each joint-angle waveform, independently per joint and limb, at 0, 2, 5, 8 and 12°; four error samples per condition and level.
Features
Camera set 33 (joint-angle range, minimum, maximum, left–right differences, timing); purely kinematic 27; with ground reaction force 51.
Classifier
Extremely randomised trees (100 trees, no tuning), leave-one-variant-out cross-validation, trained and tested at the same error level.

What a degree means here

The convention from the reliability literature treats 2° or less as acceptable, 2–5° as possibly requiring consideration and more than 5° as possibly misleading clinical interpretation (McGinley et al. 2009). Its degrees are between-session standard deviations; the degrees on this page are waveform-level RMS error. With a constant offset alone, the closest analogue of between-session error, rank-1 accuracy was 0.735 at 5° and 0.722 at 12°, against 0.654 and 0.533 for white noise in the same runs. A structured error (offset, drift and jitter) at the same total RMS gave accuracy within 0.04 of white noise from 3° to 20°.

These errors are measured against marker-based capture with different definitions, so they place the 0–12° sweep in context rather than map onto it.

Where this stops

Scope: simulation only, with no patient data; one planar model in five strength-rescaled variants, so generalisation is across overall strength, not across people; 66 analysed conditions; the severity difference and the force-plate gain at 0–2° are inconclusive at this power.

  • Not clinical accuracy. The weakness is imposed, so the answer is known; the study bounds what is identifiable in principle, not what a clinic would achieve.
  • Locating, not screening. No unimpaired condition entered the classifier, so detecting whether any weakness exists was not tested.
  • Five sagittal groups only. The model is planar, and the gluteus maximus was not weakened, so hip-extensor weakness is represented only through the hamstrings.
  • The steepest per-degree loss, between 2 and 5°, is not statistically separable from the loss between 5 and 8°.
  • The force plate's value depends on the error structure. Under a constant offset alone its gain did not grow with error (Supplement S8.2).
