Withdrawn Teachers and Learned Body Models
in Muscle-Driven Motor Learning

A competent teacher can be taken away without leaving the learner behind. A body model is worth its content: a model that knows nothing becomes more harmful the more joints there are.

Mu-Hua (Maurice) Wang · National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University

[Paper (PDF)] [Code] [All projects]

Supersedes the earlier drafts Two Routes to a Motor Prior and What a Motor Prior Is Worth. Not posted to any preprint server.

Abstract

A motor learner can be helped by a more skilled teacher or by a model of its own body learned from self-generated movement. Withdrawing a teacher removes its knowledge and changes the learner's objective at once, so whether withdrawal leaves the learner worse off, and why it drops, have not been separated; for a body model, whether imagined experience helps because of what the model has learned is rarely tested. This paper uses controls that change one thing at a time, on one soft actor-critic learner and six muscle-driven bodies: an elbow and a finger from MyoSuite and four planar arms of one to four joints. A withdrawn teacher is compared with replacing it by a frozen copy of the student, which keeps the imitation objective and removes only the teacher; a frozen body model learned from reward-free babbling is compared with a random model in identical imagination machinery. On the finger, a learner whose competent teacher is withdrawn ends ahead of one never guided, and the share of the teacher's benefit it keeps rises with the time the teacher was attached, from about a quarter after 2,000 steps to about all of it after 7,000. There, after 4,000 or more steps of attachment, most of the drop that follows withdrawal is the removed imitation term rather than the teacher's not-yet-absorbed knowledge. On planar arms, a random model, whose predictions barely depend on the action, becomes increasingly harmful as joints are added; the babble-trained model avoids this harm.

Taking a teacher away

Withdrawing a teacher changes two things at once: the learner loses the teacher's knowledge, and its training objective changes. The control is a self-anchor: at withdrawal the teacher is replaced by a frozen copy of the student itself, so the imitation term stays and only the teacher is gone.

No deficit against never being guided. On myoFinger the learner whose competent teacher is withdrawn ends −43.25 [−54.72, −31.79] mm ahead of a learner never guided. The share of the teacher's benefit it keeps rises with the time the teacher was attached, from about a quarter after 2,000 steps to about all of it after 7,000; averaged over 2,000–8,000 it is more than half, under both endpoints. On myoElbow, where the teacher is worth little, its benefit goes with it, but the withdrawn learner is not detectably behind a never-guided one, also when trained to 200k.

A body model is worth its content

The control is a random model in the identical imagination machinery. Its predictions barely depend on the action, so its imagined experience tells the learner that actions do not matter. On a series of planar arms, that kind of imagination costs little on one joint and becomes destructive as joints are added; the model learned from babbling avoids the cost. Against model-free learning with the same real data per update, the trained model's own advantage is not shown to grow with joint count.

Findings and their boundaries

The primary results hold under two endpoints (mean over 90–100k and the single 100k evaluation) and survive a Holm correction. Paired-t 95 % intervals; a non-significant cell is not detected, never equal.

Scope statements that travel with every number above

  • Single-task reach in simulation, on deterministic, fully observed bodies; the imagined reward uses exact forward kinematics.
  • Along the arm series, joint count, muscle count and biarticular muscles change together, and the one-joint arm cannot reach half of its held-out targets.
  • Each body has one teacher; the finger's competent teacher is the best of twelve candidate runs. Withdrawal is a single step at 2k–8k steps, and guided-phase transitions stay in the replay buffer.

Method

One soft actor-critic learner in every condition (two hidden layers of 128, batch 128, update-to-data ratio 2), twelve matched seeds per condition (twenty-four on the three- and four-joint arms), evaluation on twenty held-out targets disjoint from the eight training targets, 100,000 training steps (the elbow withdrawal also to 200,000). The teacher is a frozen actor joined through an imitation term; its control at withdrawal is the self-anchor. The body model is a forward model learned once from reward-free Ornstein–Uhlenbeck babbling, frozen, and used for short Dyna rollouts; its control is a random model in the same loop. 1,056 runs, about 1,100 single-thread CPU-hours.
