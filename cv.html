Research interest

Physical assistive robotics for long-term care. I want to build robots that carry out contact-rich care tasks on a bedridden human body — starting with diaper changing, a task that demands three things at once: perceiving a body that will not cooperate, predicting how that body moves when it is touched, and manipulating deformable material safely against skin.

My work so far covers all three. I have built and clinically validated a markerless human motion capture system, written learned dynamics models of musculoskeletal bodies, and measured whether compositional motion priors survive transfer to a robot hand. I am looking for a lab where these can be assembled into a machine that does the task.

Oct 2025 – present

Robot learning and embodied AI — independent research

Sole author on all three manuscripts

A self-directed programme on one question: how does an agent acquire a model of a body, and what does that model actually buy? Design, implementation, experiments, statistics and writing done independently. Every result carries adversarial controls and multiple-comparison correction, and the negative findings are reported as findings.

  • Two Routes to a Motor Prior: Guidance versus a Frozen Body Model. A frozen babble-learned forward model against a distilled teacher policy under one SAC backbone on MyoSuite bodies, with seven controls and a matched withdrawal. Guidance buys the early speed; on myoFinger that lead reverses inside the budget. n = 12 matched seeds, Holm–Bonferroni over six stated family definitions.
  • Which Hand-Motion Composition Axes Are Worth Measuring. Built the measurement modular motion priors assume but rarely test: a paired split over six axes of three hand datasets, behind five gates. Difficulty appears only where the two factors interact — and the paper's own negative control removed its strongest result.
  • What Master-Then-Reorganise Does Not Buy: An Invariance Argument for Body Codes. An algebraic identity rules out a whole family of “reorganise the code” proposals before any experiment runs; the two conditions it needs are then measured. A re-encoding that preserves decodability by construction still costs +0.875 mm — a counterexample to decodability as a proxy for representation quality.

Jan 2023 – present

Circular-geometry representation of behavioural trajectories

Taoyuan General Hospital · Advisor: Dr. Tso-Jeng Wang

A methods problem disguised as a clinical one: the discriminating feature of a dose trajectory is its shape, but shape is confounded with magnitude and lives on a space that Euclidean clustering gets wrong.

  • Built the representation. Normalised each daily consumed-dose series to its own value seven days earlier, took the extreme three-day moving-window slopes on each side of the test, and mapped the (post, pre) slope pair to a polar angle θ = atan2(post, pre). Shape becomes comparable independent of absolute magnitude, and the feature space becomes a torus.
  • Clustered on the manifold, not in the plane. Fitted a bivariate (product) von Mises mixture by EM on the circle. For the stable-dose branch, reduced 114 variables by PCA and cut a circular kernel-density estimate at its valleys. Nine patterns emerged, spanning positive/negative occurrence ratios 1.64 to 0.50.
  • Attacked my own result. Outcome-blind re-clustering, 500 bootstrap refits per branch, within-patient conditional logistic regression, restriction to windows containing no interpolated day, and reporting across K = 4–8 because K was an investigator choice and no criterion selected it.
  • Reported what did not survive. Post-test escalation magnitude (OR 1.36 → 1.06 within patients) is not claimed; the stable-dose branch is labelled exploratory; the association did not replicate across a temporal split; and the trajectory adds essentially nothing predictive over attendance. Pre-test escalation did survive, and strengthened (1.15 → 1.26 per SD, p = 3 × 10−7).
  • Scale: 9,900 analysable urine tests from 552 patients over 2018–2022, from a 20,108-record extract; an earlier phase covered 1,000+ patients from the Taiwan National Health Insurance Database.
