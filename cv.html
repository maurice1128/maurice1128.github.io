Research interest

Robots for long-term care. I want to build robots that carry out contact-rich care tasks on a person who cannot cooperate, ending with diaper changing. I came to this from two years of clinical motion capture in hospitals and day-care centres, and my questions come from there: what a robot must know about a person before it moves them, and what its sensors can tell it at the moment of contact.

Oct 2025 – present

Contact Safety for Care Robots — independent, sole author

Gentle by knowing: person knowledge protects restricted joints better than force thresholds in simulated robotic arm lifting — Frontiers in Robotics and AI: peer review completed, both reviewers endorsed publication

  • When a robot supports a limb from outside, the loaded joint is not on the axis of its force sensor. The tightest force guard lowered mean joint load by 29–36% but changed harmful outcomes by only 0–7 points. With a complete care record, a policy learned offline cut harmful outcomes from 110 to 25 of 300 new simulated persons, mainly by declining 91; replicated on 300 more (−0.27 [−0.32, −0.21]).

Do Not Stop at First Contact: deliberate overshoot makes a care robot's pain risk robust to its noise model — submitted, IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters

  • A robot that stops at first resistance has one reading, at its sensor's detection floor. Pushing 2.4° further keeps a stated 5% pain risk at 5.0–6.3% while the assumed noise ranges up to seven times the truth, against 6.7–17.1% for stopping at contact. The cost: more simulated persons hurt while probing.

Teaching

Feb – Aug 2026

Physical Science teacher, Grade 8

Tamkang High School, New Taipei

Taught the full Grade-8 physical science curriculum to two of the grade's eight classes.

  • Class 8 finished 2nd of eight classes on the third term exam, up from 3rd, with its mean margin over the school average widening from +5.1 to +11.8 points across three term exams.
  • The gain came from the bottom of the distribution, not the top. By the third exam, Class 8's bottom-quartile cutoff (78) had reached the school-wide median, and its bottom-12 % cutoff stood 28 points above the school's — up from a 14-point gap on the first exam.
  • Class 8 held the second-narrowest score spread in the grade (SD 15.07 against a school-wide 21.86): the class improved by pulling up students who were behind, not by stretching students already ahead.
  • Designed the mechanism that produced it. The class earned its reward only if every student scored 100 — a 10-minute paper, then open discussion, then a fresh paper, repeated until the whole class cleared it. The collective condition put the strongest students to work teaching the weakest, which is where the measured gain came from.
  • Ran self-initiated weekly remedial sessions outside class hours across three grades, targeted at the students who were behind — not assigned by the school.
  • Ran hands-on chemistry and physics activities outside the syllabus, and class events to build cohesion.
