# Audit: sections 4 (Black Death), 5 (Slave religion), 8 (Continental)

Independent fact-check, 2026-11-05. The section files were not edited. Method: Crossref API for every DOI in the three reference lists (58 of 58 resolved), DNB SRU, BnF SRU, Open Library, and publisher, review and open-access pages via web search and fetch. Where a full text was available (Dittmar and Meisenzahl 2020; Jedwab, Johnson and Koyama 2017 WP and 2022 WP; Finley and Koyama WP; Douglass 1845), the claim was checked against that text.

Headline: **no fabricated works were found.** Every cited work that was sampled exists, and all 58 DOIs resolve to the stated work. The main problems are one clear misattribution (Schwartz 1982), two misleading paraphrases (Finley and Koyama 2018; Nissi and Testoni 2025), and several quotations attributed to authors that come from blurbs or reviews.

## B. Items checked and found correct (coverage)

### Numbers, dates and direct quotations (highest risk)
- **Dittmar and Meisenzahl 2020**: plague outbreaks 1500–1522 used as an instrument; controls for 1350–1499 outbreaks; "each additional plague outbreak between 1500 and 1522 increases the propensity of adopting a law by 14 percentage points"; mechanism (elites discredited, public-goods salience). Source: published PDF, ReStud 87(2): 959–96, pp. 968 and Table 6 (https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/91195/). OK.
- **Jedwab, Johnson and Koyama 2017 WP**: protective effect "weaker in cities first infected in January or April-May ... (Epiphany and Easter) ... stronger for December or February–March ... Advent and Lent." Source: MPRA 78630, p. 3. OK.
- **Jedwab, Johnson and Koyama 2019**: higher mortality made persecution less likely; complementarity. Source: MPRA abstract and JEG record. OK.
- **Jedwab, Johnson and Koyama 2022**: "a decline in the authority of religious institutions"; "inability of religious leaders to offer protection ... (see Johnson and Koyama, 2019, p. 110-112)". Found verbatim in the WP versions (AEA asset 12804; GWU IIEP WP 2020-14). Not checked against the paywalled JEL print text. OK.
- **Archambeau 2021**: sixty-eight testimonies, 1363 canonisation inquest of Delphine de Puimichel at Apt; witnesses had lived through 1348 and 1361. OK.
- **Wingard 2026**: moral reform and liturgy as the core of plague management; restraint of lust, especially sodomy; 1348–1400. Source: sciencia.cat abstract; Crossref JMH 52(2): 253–72. OK.
- **L. Jones 2022** quote "what, where, and especially who was blamed": verbatim in the McGill-Queen's description. OK. (It is a blurb quote; consider marking it as such.)
- **Steinhoff 2006** "profoundly and intentionally pluralistic": quoted in Southard's review, *Renaissance Quarterly* 60(3). OK.
- **Wray 2009** "more than a thousand" testaments: Brill description ("over a thousand testaments"). Medieval Mediterranean 83 also confirmed. OK.
- **Cohn 1992**: six cities (Arezzo, Assisi, Florence, Perugia, Pisa, Siena); 1363 turning point; bequests concentrated rather than divided. OK. (Section 8's [GK] on the 1363 thesis can be removed.)
- **Cohn 2007** "the most monumental of medieval Jewish persecutions": Project MUSE excerpt. OK.
- **Barker 2007**: the plague cult of Sebastian postdates 1348; chapter at **pp. 90–131** (resolves the [verify]). OK.
- **Clement VI**: condemnation of the flagellants dated 20 October 1349; *Quamvis perfidiam* July 1348 (6 July). Confirmed by several sources. The bull title "Inter sollicitudines" was not confirmed by these searches.
- **Robinson 2019**: 1349 ordinands "far exceeded the annual average"; rest of the 1350s "less than half of the pre-plague number"; similar social background. OK.
- **Mengel 2011**: "For the kingdom as a whole, the 1380 plague epidemic proved far more damaging." OK.
- **Douglass 1845** quotation: verbatim against the Gutenberg text. The section quotes two fragments, both exact. OK.
- **Fountain 2010**: "fewer than 40 percent of individuals who gave a datable conversion experience had become Christians prior to acquiring freedom." Source: LSU Press description. Note that the press says "both the survey and the narratives," so "Using WPA narratives" is slightly narrow. OK.
- **Schweninger 2011**: "provocative and interesting study ... the great majority [of] slaves ... did not accept Christ." Source: CWBR. OK.
- **Boles 2020**: "records of over four hundred congregations." OK.
- **Stowell 1998**: "the Black quest for religious autonomy" is from the ch. 5 title, "Crossing Jordan: The Black Quest for Religious Autonomy." OK.
- **Pierce 2005**: five autobiographers (White, Jea, Smith, Bayley, Elaw); "New Jerusalem." OK.
- **Rucker 2006**: coverage from the 1712 New York revolt to Nat Turner in 1831. OK.
- **AME membership** (20,000 in 1856; 70,000 in 1866; 207,000 in 1876): matches figures widely reproduced from the *Annual Cyclopaedia* (Wikipedia, "African Methodist Episcopal Church"). The primary source was not checked, so the existing [verify] stands.
- **J. Butler 1990** "African spiritual holocaust": the chapter is "Slavery and the African Spiritual Holocaust." OK (resolves the [verify] on wording).
- **Noordegraaf and Valk**: "in de periode 1450–1668, een tijdvak van 219 jaar, ... in 107 jaren" (DBNL full text). OK.
