The article asks how people respond toward faith and toward the church after collective suffering. It compares two settings across time and distance: plague-era England and the slave South of the United States. It rests on three datasets coded for this study from published primary sources: 1,979 wills enrolled in London's Court of Husting between 1320 and 1400, 509 parish gilds recorded in the royal inquiry of 1388–89, and a stratified random sample of 400 Works Progress Administration ex-slave narratives. From these and from contemporary texts, it induces a typology of responses organized around one question: who holds the remedy that a community's theology prescribes against suffering and death.
