### Research question and thesis

Taiwan offers the series' closest approach to a natural experiment (section 07). Between the 228 Incident of 1947 and the end of martial law in 1987, one party-state ruled Protestant and Catholic churches whose people stood in very different relations to it. The Presbyterian Church in Taiwan (PCT) worshipped in Taiwanese, Hakka and Indigenous languages, and many of its members belonged to the population that suffered most under the regime. Members, and reportedly clergy, were among the victims of the White Terror *[verify: section 07 states this without a source, and Rubinstein is cited there only for the Protestant spectrum and Minnan/Hakka selfhood; document it from TJDB clergy and church-worker cases]*. The anticommunist churches that Tseng (2017) calls "party-state Christians" (黨國基督徒) aligned with the regime. The Catholic Church grew more than sevenfold within ten years of 1949, much of it among Mainlander refugees and others in crisis, while it distributed US surplus flour, milk and clothing (Madsen 2012). The article asks whether the church's role, rather than the suffering itself, decided whether suffering raised devotion and trust together.

The thesis has three parts. (1) **Own-community effect:** after the PCT's public turn (PCT 1971, 1975, 1977), its Hoklo and Hakka congregations should show retention and devotion at or above trend through the repression of 1977–1980, including the imprisonment of General Secretary Kao Chun-ming, arrested in 1980 after the 1979 Kaohsiung Incident *[add a citation: a PCT source, or Rubinstein 2003 after checking]*. They should not show the losses that fear alone would predict. (2) **Alignment effect:** party-state churches should show no comparable link between repression episodes and devotion. Their reading of the persecution of Presbyterians as punishment for political activity, not for faith (Worthy News n.d. *[verify; single informant]*), should appear in their own press. (3) **Provision effect:** Catholic growth and decline should follow the timing of relief, and of dependence on the mission church, more closely than the timing of suffering (Madsen 2012).

The claim can be refuted in four ways. It fails if PCT growth after 1971 comes almost entirely from Indigenous presbyteries. The WCC profile reports that the Ten Plus One Movement, launched in 1978, added some 80 churches and about 40,000 members, so this test needs those figures broken down by presbytery (WCC n.d.). It fails if Hoklo and Hakka PCT membership fell during repression faster than party-state church membership. It fails if PCT loyalty follows electoral or nationalist mobilisation and not repression episodes, which would support the political-alignment rival (Chiu Kai-li 2021). It fails if the Catholic decline began before relief was reduced, or followed Vatican II and the language barrier rather than relief. The PCT's 1990 apology admits that under martial law "the church as a whole did not give the victims' families the support they deserved" (PCT 1990). That admission makes the period before 1971 a within-church baseline: the same constituency, under a different church role. Part of that baseline is rapid growth: membership and congregations roughly doubled in 1955–65, around the Double the Church movement before the 1965 centenary (WCC n.d.). That growth came before the 1971 turn, so it is a baseline fact and not evidence for the own-community effect.

The comparison concerns human rights, democratisation and disaster ministry. It does not concern sexual ethics. The PCT's pastoral letter of 10 June 2014 defined marriage as the union of one man and one woman, called churches to accept people of same-sex orientation and stated that same-sex sexual acts are contrary to the Bible (PCT 2014). On marriage the PCT is therefore not the opposite pole to the conservative coalition of 2018 (Huang Ke-hsien 2017; Ho 2019). The article should say this in its scope statement and stay neutral on that issue and on Taiwan's constitutional future.

### Design and evidence

**Case selection.** The three arms hold the state, the era (1947–1990), the island and broadly the Christian tradition roughly constant. What varies is the church's role: the PCT as the sufferers' own community from 1971 (and a silent one before), the party-state churches as allies of the perpetrator state, and the Catholic Church as relief provider. One confound must be stated openly: the sufferer constituency also varies, since Hoklo and Hakka members predominate in the PCT and Mainlanders in the party-state and refugee congregations. The design reduces this confound in three ways. First, a within-PCT comparison before and after 1971 keeps the constituency fixed and varies the role. Second, the early TSCS religion waves can show the affiliation of Taiwanese-origin respondents outside the PCT, if an item on provincial origin exists *[verify item list]*. The full TSCS religion series is 1985, 1994, 1999, 2004, 2009, 2014, 2018 and 2023; this article uses the 1985, 1994 and 1999 waves, and Article 4 uses 2004–2023. The 2018 report's cross-wave table lists 1994 and 1999 as the earlier religion waves; whether the 1990 survey carries religion items is unverified. Third, Catholic Mainlander refugees provide a test of provision that does not depend on ethnicity. This is a controlled comparison in Slater and Ziblatt's (2013) sense, combined with process tracing (Mahoney 2012). It is not a natural experiment.

**Measures.** The article must keep devotion and institutional trust apart. Bottan and Perez-Truglia (2015) show that participation and belief can move separately.

**The Indigenous strand.** After 1945, whole Indigenous villages converted. Christians made up 85.13 percent of mountain Indigenous people in 1985 and 83.94 percent in 1991, while lowland figures fell from 70.70 to 61.43 percent (Fan Chiang 2019). Explanations include social crisis (Huang Ying-kuei 1991), forced relocation followed by a "changed heart" discourse that damped conflict (Wang Mei-hsia 2018), continuity with ancestral spirit relations (Chiu Yun-fang 2004, 2013 *[verify 2013/2014]*), and the PCT's mountain mission working alongside the state (Chou 2015; Sia 2018). In the recommended scope this strand matters chiefly as a confound: PCT totals must be decomposed so that Indigenous growth is not mistaken for a response to persecution. The claim that people converted for relief goods ("flour religion", 麵粉教) has **no scholarly source** (section 07). It may be tested but must not be asserted.

**Narrowing options.**
- **(a) Recommended: the PCT vs. party-state churches, 1965–1990, with the Catholic case as a provision test.** The window runs from the 1965 centenary conflict (Tseng 2017) to the 1990 apology, with 1947–1964 as background and the pre-1971 baseline. It is the cleanest test of church role and fits 8,000–10,000 words.
- **(b) Indigenous conversion, 1945–1970, and the later decline.** This is anthropologically rich and largely in Chinese. It suits 《臺灣史研究》 or a Chinese-language companion article, and it tests the timing hypothesis (Fountain 2010).
- **(c) Both strands in one article.** Strongest substantively, but it needs at least 12,000 words, fits only a long-format venue and risks thin treatment of each strand.

### Risks and fallbacks

- **The yearbooks are incomplete or not broken down by presbytery.** Fall back on presbytery reports in *Taiwan Church News* and any figures in Rubinstein (1991) *[verify]*; narrow the claim to qualitative process tracing.
- **The party-state arm is thin.** Rely on Tseng (2017) and the periodicals he cites, and recast the article as a within-PCT study before and after 1971, with the Catholic case as a foil.
- **Catholic and relief records cannot be reached.** Use Madsen (2012) as the published test and cut section 6 to about 600 words.
- **The PCT figures do not support the claim** (for example, Hoklo/Hakka membership fell during repression). Report it. A refutation of the own-community effect in the series' best-controlled case is publishable and would reshape the model.
- **The TJDB occupation coding cannot identify clergy.** Search by name using PCT and laijohn.com lists.
- **Contested politics.** Use neutral terms ("party-state", "self-determination statements"), quote positions without endorsing them, and keep the marriage question out of scope (PCT 2014).
