## 19. Research gaps, contribution and journal fit

The review shows that scholars agree collective suffering raises demand for meaning, intercession and community, but no account predicts where that demand goes. The series answers with one variable, who controls the remedy, coded apart from outcomes. Every part of the framework has prior art, which the articles must credit. Each article's first-choice venue allows AI-drafted text with disclosure; Article 3's earlier first and second choices are now conditional.

### 19.1 Gaps the review establishes

Ten gaps recur across the sections. Each is tied to the sections that show it and to the article that addresses it.

1. **Demand without a destination.** Deprivation, coping and insecurity theories explain why suffering raises religious demand, not which body receives it. Authority theories explain which institution gains but do not model shocks, and power-and-meaning theories treat "the church" as one actor (section 2; 15.1). Articles 1 and 2.
2. **Faith and institutional trust are rarely measured apart in history.** Bequests, ordinations and membership counts load on both (section 14.7). The split has been measured after scandal (Bottan and Perez-Truglia 2015) and, for one contemporary war, in survey data (Ishchuk 2026), but not for historical collective suffering (section 18). Articles 1, 2 and 4.
3. **Provision of care is conflated with control of the remedy.** Care and insurance accounts explain church growth by what churches deliver (Stark 1996; Ager and Ciccone 2018). Belloc, Drago, and Galbiati (2016) show that earthquakes delayed self-government where bishops ruled and that those cities then built and decorated more churches; that the bishop's monopoly of mediation entrenched him is their reading, which this supports but does not establish. No study tests who provides against who controls (sections 15.4, 18.5). Articles 2 and 3.
4. **Church role is coded from outcomes.** If "own church" is inferred from loyalty, the argument restates its result. Outcome-independent indicators (ordination, property, rites, funds, documented care) have not been assembled for these cases (section 15.3). Articles 2 and 3.
5. **Power position and church role are confounded.** In the US South the enslaved were subordinate and also served by churches allied with their masters (section 5). Brazil's brotherhoods, Rwanda and the Presbyterian Church in Taiwan (PCT) separate the two, but no study compares cases on that axis (sections 6, 7, 15.2). Articles 2 and 3.
6. **Timing is unresolved.** Fountain (2010) dates most datable conversions after emancipation, yet 26 of 51 coded WPA narratives report Black-led worship before 1865 (section 5). On the plague side, durable testamentary change is reported after the 1363 recurrence, not 1348 (Cohn 1992 *[verify at first hand]*). Duration and recurrence have not been tested as conditions (sections 4, 14.6). Article 2.
7. **Redistribution and exit are not distinguished.** Prior work asks whether faith survives a shock. It does not ask why devotion sometimes moves to lay bodies inside a church, as with confraternities after 1348, and sometimes leaves for sufferer-controlled bodies, as with the AME and Black Baptists after 1865 (sections 4, 5, 18.5). Article 2.
8. **The loss-of-faith branch is thinly documented.** Black humanist theodicy defines it (Jones [1973] 1998; Pinn 1995), but evidence before 1877 is sparse, and belief is seldom measured apart from belonging (sections 3, 15.5). Article 1 maps the gap; no article tests it yet.
9. **Non-Western and Sinophone evidence is missing.** No Chinese-language study compares collective suffering across cases or asks when it erodes church trust (sections 9, 18.5). Taiwan offers three churches under one regime, 1947–1990, and survey trust items for 2009–2018 (sections 7, 17). Articles 3 and 4.
10. **Transmission is assumed rather than tested outside the West.** Belief without a gathered community decays across generations in Britain (Voas and Crockett 2005). Whether that holds where Christians are a small minority is unknown (sections 2, 15.5). Article 4.

### 19.2 Contribution of the series

The series contributes a mechanism, not the discovery that religious responses to shocks are conditional. Economists have already shown conditionality (Belloc, Drago, and Galbiati 2016; Dittmar and Meisenzahl 2020). The defensible novelty is the combination of variables, its application to historical collective suffering, and the distinction between redistribution and exit (section 18.1). Claims should read "to our knowledge, no study has", not "the first", until the database searches in section 18.7 are run.

Martin (1978) and Casanova (1994) are the closest precedent for the church-role variable. They work at the level of whole societies and long-run secularization and do not model shocks, so the series' addition is the shock and the sub-national institution (section 2). Weber is the largest prior-art risk: reviewers will say stratum-specific theodicy is classical. The articles should cite him early and claim only the institutional variable and the split outcome (section 18.6).

### 19.4 AI-use disclosure principle

A disclosure must describe what was actually done, no more and no less. This project used AI for literature discovery, bibliographic verification, summarising sources, and drafting notes and article designs. If AI drafts any article text, the author uses version (b): it names the sections drafted, the tool, version and dates, and states that the author rewrote the text and checked every claim against sources read personally. The sentence "I read every work cited" belongs in a statement only if it is true on the day of submission. Where version (b) is banned or high-risk (*Past & Present*, JAS, RRCS, SWC, and Chinese journals until their editors confirm), the author either writes the text personally or chooses another venue. The disclosure is never trimmed to fit the venue. A dated log of tools, versions and tasks, kept from now on, makes each statement accurate.

### Implications for the model

- The model should be presented as a mechanism for a conditionality economists have already shown, with "who controls the remedy" as its distinctive variable.
- Gaps 3 and 4 make outcome-independent coding of church role the first methodological task in Articles 2 and 3.
- The redistribution-versus-exit distinction (outcomes B and C) is the series' most defensible new outcome claim; the faith/trust split itself is credited to prior work.
- Taiwan supplies the only within-state test of church role against power position (Article 3) and the only non-Western test of transmission (Article 4).
- The loss-of-faith branch remains untested; Article 1 should state it as an open question, not a finding.
