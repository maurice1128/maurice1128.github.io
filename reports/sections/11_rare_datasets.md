## 11. Rare datasets and specialized archives

The working model can be tested, not just illustrated, because each case has at least one serial, person- or place-level source. These sources record devotion and institutional affiliation separately, so the two can be measured apart. They include sacramental registers of Afro-descendants in Catholic slave societies, papal and episcopal registers spanning 1348, county-level US denominational censuses, and a 9,915-case database of Taiwan's political prosecutions.

This section lists only resources whose pages or records were opened on 5 November 2026. Each was checked by an HTTP 200 page read, a repository API (Harvard Dataverse, Zenodo, Library of Congress JSON, Internet Archive), a Crossref or doi.org record, or a Wayback snapshot. Entries checked only by a title page or a Wayback snapshot are marked "existence only". Resources that could not be opened are listed under "To verify". Full verification notes are in `research_notes/Collective suffering and Christian faith/rare_datasets_archives.md`.

### Black Death and late medieval Europe

The medieval case needs two kinds of data. One measures exposure: where plague struck and how often. The other measures response: bequests, ordinations, petitions and persecutions. Exposure data are now open, but they inherit Biraben's undocumented sources. Roosen and Curtis (2018) show that studies built on the Biraben catalogue report anywhere from 5,559 to 7,711 outbreaks depending on scope. Any test of "recurrence rather than 1348" (Cohn) must treat missing records as missing, not as absence of plague.

### Enslaved and freed African Americans

The US sources can measure both outcomes the model predicts. Testimony records intensified personal faith alongside distrust of the masters' church. Denominational statistics record the post-emancipation move into Black-controlled bodies. The ARDA county files and the Methodist conference minutes allow a direct test of Fountain's (2010) claim that mass affiliation came largely after 1865.

### Brazil and the Caribbean

Brazil and Cuba can separate the two variables the US case confounds. There, the church that baptized the enslaved was also the slaveholders' church, and Black lay brotherhoods (irmandades, cabildos) gave the enslaved some institutional control inside it. The Slave Societies Digital Archive is the decisive source. It holds sacramental registers that show who was baptized, married and buried, and who stood as godparent, across four centuries.

### Taiwan

The Taiwan case is unusually well documented on both sides of the model. State-violence databases give person-level exposure. The Presbyterian Church in Taiwan (PCT) left a continuous press record since 1885 and public statements from the martial-law era. Survey data then track religion and trust after democratization. Together these could test whether a church that sided with the sufferers raised devotion and institutional loyalty together. The likely contrast is churches aligned with the Kuomintang state.

### Contemporary religion surveys

Modern surveys can measure the divergence the model predicts: belief holding steady while confidence in churches falls, especially after scandals where the church caused the suffering. Gallup's confidence in "the church or organized religion" stood at 33 percent in 2026, down from 42 percent in 2020.

### Ten priority datasets

1. Slave Societies Digital Archive: the only serial record that separates power position from church role.
2. Taiwan Transitional Justice Database: person-level state-violence exposure that can identify clergy.
3. Taiwan Social Change Survey religion modules: nine waves from 1985 to 2023.
4. Elder John Lai's Archives: codable PCT and Indigenous-church histories.
5. Digitized Biraben outbreaks, with the Krauer–Schmid and Bauch–Büntgen supplements, read through Roosen and Curtis.
6. York Archbishops' Registers: ordinations, chantries and wills across 1348 and 1361.
7. Calendar of Papal Registers vol. 3 with the Court of Husting wills.
8. Doten-Snitker expulsions data with the Gingerich–Vogler outbreak files.
9. ARDA Census of Religious Bodies county files with the Methodist conference minutes.
10. *The Christian Recorder* with the Last Seen advertisements.
